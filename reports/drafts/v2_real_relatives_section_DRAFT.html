DRAFT v2 section (for review; not for deposit): real related languages written in different texts

Status: draft written 2026-10-20, revised 2026-10-21 after an audit for oversights, on branch v2, from the reports named in each paragraph. It does not change any v1 number or the v1 paper (paper-v1). Every number here is a v2 result, and every decimal in it is checked against a generated report (reports/v2/cd/analysis/check_numbers.py). Methods are frozen at frozen-v2 (commit 4ce3e90); pre-registration entries 1-8 are in docs/v2_preregistration.md. Each claim is labelled pre-registered, exploratory or post hoc. Nothing here is, or claims, a decipherment.

Key findings

  • 0 of 12 cells (3 real relatives × 4 script types) reach a 50% mean, on either seed block. Pre-registered (entry 6) and replicated on fresh seeds (entry 7).
  • 2 of 12 cells show the relative beating an unrelated language by the rule fixed before that control ran: Kannada, alphabetic (+15.1 points) and Malayalam, syllabic (+7.6). Italian never does. Entry 8, C1.
  • 14.0-19.7% of alphabetic tokens are recovered with an unrelated Finnish reference, scored against the relatives' own gold, and a constant answer gets 14.2-15.8%. An unrelated reference thus reaches more than half of the 20.3-34.4% that a real relative reaches (the shares, not necessarily the same tokens). Entry 8, C1 and C2.
  • +11.5 points for Old Tamil-Malayalam, alphabetic, with 10 EM restarts instead of 3 (30.4% to 41.9%): the only first-order budget effect. All 120 reruns at 3 restarts reproduce the frozen records exactly. Entry 8, C3 and C4.
  • Bracket failed for Latin: Italian scores below the synthetic harsh setting on both seed blocks, although it is closer to Latin on every yardstick, and more restarts widen the gap (harsh 43.6%, Italian 18.1%). Post hoc, negative result 3.
  • 0.0% logographic recovery with every real relative, below a constant answer (8.6-17.5%). The solver can only output the reference's 250 most frequent words, and the hidden language's frequent words have rare cognates: Latin et is rank 2,592 in Dante. Exploratory.

v2.1 Why real relatives

The v1 related tier used a synthetic sister cut from the other half of the hidden language's own source text. It shares phrases with the hidden text and keeps its vocabulary, morphology and word order, so it is far closer than any attested relative of an undeciphered script is likely to be (v1, Section 3.6, Table 2). Stage 2 first tried to make the synthetic sister realistic by calibrating it to the distance between Ugaritic and Hebrew, the classic real case for computational decipherment (Snyder, Barzilay & Knight 2010). That failed for all five languages in both settings tried: every mechanism that changes letter pairs also destroys shared word forms, and a sister cut from the same text keeps too much verbatim text (docs/v2_negative_results.md, item 2). Stage 2 therefore measures real related languages written in different texts, with no tuning and no targets: whatever distance each real pair has is the distance used (pre-registration entry 5).

Data notes.

  • The two Wikisource transcriptions state no source edition. They were accepted as clean by the project owner (2026-10-15): they carry the CC BY-SA site licence and do not link to the CC BY-NC-ND scan on Internet Archive.
  • Kannada coverage is partial: 93 of the 154 sandhi pages linked from the ten parva pages exist, mostly Ādi, Virāṭa and Karṇa parvas. The two commentary (ಸಟೀಕಾ) pages are excluded. Each sandhi page opens with a short invocation that may be in Sanskrit; it was not removed.
  • Revision ids and SHA-256 hashes per page are kept with the raw data.

Registers, periods and sizes differ across pairs, and none of this is controlled.

  • Register. Both hidden languages are literary registers (Classical Latin prose and verse; Sangam poetry), while each relative descends from, or is related to, a spoken form. Italian continues Vulgar, not Classical, Latin (Grandgent 1907: §§3-4).
  • Period. The relatives are written many centuries after the hidden texts: Dante (14th century) after Caesar and Vergil; Ezhuthachan (16th century) and Kumāravyāsa (15th century) after the Sangam anthologies. Kumāravyāsa's Kannada shows the change of initial p to h that Caldwell calls "comparatively recent" (1875: 53): in our text hattu 'ten' occurs 28 times and pattu once. The gold keeps p as the regular reflex and h as attested, so strict scoring is conservative for Kannada.
  • Loans. Caldwell (1875: 23) notes that Sanskrit loans are fewest in Tamil and most numerous in Malayalam; our Old Tamil texts are Sangam poetry, with few.
  • Reference size. Italian has 391,950 units, Malayalam 479,851 and Kannada 857,617. The v1 Latin and Tamil references, cut from the same half of the source as their synthetic sisters, have 118,647 and 502,785 alphabetic units (reports/v2/ugaritic/results.md). Reference size may affect the solver; it is not controlled.

Units and spelling. Units are those of the v1 hidden language: Latin letters by the v1 greedy segmentation, and Old Tamil phonemes. Malayalam and Kannada script are converted to the same phonemes by deterministic tables, tested on hand-checked words. Every pair is measured as written and normalised, and the normalised value is primary, so that spelling conventions do not count as language distance (the Hebrew lesson of v2 entry 3):

  • Italian: accents stripped, j folded to i, silent h dropped, apostrophes split words.
  • Malayalam and Kannada: the voicing and aspiration that Tamil script cannot write are collapsed (kh, g, gh → k, and so on).

Gold tables. Each hidden unit has a regular reflex (its outcome where no conditioned change applies) and a list of attested reflexes, with the section or page of the source for every row. Rows the source does not cover are marked "no source" and are unrecoverable under strict scoring (Old Tamil h, s, ḵ, ṣ for both Dravidian relatives). Both sources are over a century old; their basic consonant correspondences are standard, but details are dated. No gold value comes from Wikipedia. On 2026-10-21 four rows were spot-checked against the scanned texts, and each citation holds: Kannada initial p > h (Caldwell 1875: 53), talei = Malayalam tala (p. 19), Malayalam as an offshoot of Tamil (pp. 23-24) and Latin x > s (Grandgent 1907: §255). This is a spot check, not a full audit.

v2.3 How far the real relatives are

The Ugaritic-Hebrew yardsticks (v2 anchor) are computed with the same code on each pair. Sample sizes are matched to the anchor, and chance is estimated from shuffled correspondence maps.

Cross-script comparisons are indicative only. Shared word forms and word pairs are not directly comparable between a consonant-only script (Ugaritic-Hebrew) and scripts that write vowels (all the other rows): a consonant skeleton matches far more easily than a full word, even after the chance correction. Comparisons within one script type are the meaningful ones.

For Latin-Italian and Malayalam the plug-in estimate of shared word forms lies above its own bootstrap interval, because resampling duplicates lines and so lowers the share of distinct forms; the interval is the conservative reading.

Reading.

  • On these yardsticks the real relatives sit near the harsh setting and far from the v1-like sister, which shares 4-12 times as many word forms.
  • Malayalam is closer to Old Tamil than Kannada on the letter-pair distance, and the intervals do not overlap. That fits the relationship labels, but the two texts also differ in genre, period and size, so the distance cannot be attributed to the relationship alone.
  • Normalisation matters most where scripts differ most. Kannada writes voicing; collapsing it moves the letter-pair distance from 0.309 to 0.180 bits.

What the verbatim matches are (exploratory inspection, reports/v2/real_relatives/verbatim.md). Verbatim runs of 6+ units occur in 1.4% (Malayalam), 1.6% (Kannada) and 2.5% (Italian) of sampled windows, against 0.0-0.1% through shuffled maps and 0.3-0.4% for an unrelated language with a similar open syllable shape (the v1 Finnish hidden half, letters as they are). They are therefore signal, but not shared text:

  • 60-73% of them run across a word boundary;
  • they are mostly short shared morphs and phoneme strings (for example Malayalam -kkoṇṭu, colliyatu, paricil), and for Italian, cognate stems and Dante's Latinisms (principi-, cantando, crudel-);
  • no match is a phrase that respects word boundaries on both sides, so they are not shared formulae.

In Malayalam about a quarter of the matches (35 of 143) exist only after normalisation. By inspection, most of these pair a native Tamil string with a Sanskrit loanword: the loan is a coincidental partner, not a shared loan.

v2.4 Tasks C and D with real relatives

Design (entries 1, 2, 4-6).

  • Corpora: the v1 Indus-point corpora (2,906 texts, mean length 4.6 signs), seeds 0-2, samplers A and B, four script types, with the corrected (content-split) sister: 6 corpora per hidden language and script type. They were regenerated from the same seeds; all 120 have corpus statistics identical to the v1 records (checked 2026-10-20).

  • References: the relative's whole text at the script's unit level, as v1 writes its references:

    • phonemes for alphabetic scripts;
    • CV syllables for syllabic scripts;
    • word forms for logographic scripts;
    • for logo-syllabic scripts, word forms for the relative's 250 most frequent words and syllables otherwise.

    No reference carries a correspondence table, so every score is before the cognate step.

  • Tiers: related gives the relative alone. candidates gives the relative plus the other v1 languages.

  • Methods: the frozen EM solver after Knight et al. (2006), with the revised selection rule (primary) and the original rule, and the frequency-rank baseline. Each EM fit keeps the best of 3 restarts: a frequency-informed start (sign rank k to unit rank k) and two random starts.
  • Scoring:
    • Strict (primary): a sign token is right when its predicted unit is the regular reflex of the token's hidden value. A logogram is right only when its reflex is a word that occurs in the relative (a cognate). Many-to-one predictions are accepted.
    • Lenient (secondary): any combination of attested reflexes counts.
    • Every token counts. The share of tokens that have a strict reflex at all is reported as a ceiling.
  • Bracket rows: beside the real relatives stand the v1 sister (labelled upper bound in entry 4; read from the v1 records and scored with the v1 scorer before the cognate step) and harsh (labelled lower bound in entry 4, which it is not for real relatives: negative result 3).

v2.5 Controls: is the relative doing the work?

Pre-registration entry 8 fixed four controls and their readings before they ran (post hoc in motivation). Results in reports/v2/cd/controls.md.

C1. Unrelated reference. The same corpora are run with an unrelated v1 language as the only reference, and its predictions are scored against the real relative's gold, exactly as the relative's own run. A relative helps in a cell only if it adds at least 5 points over the better unrelated reference and is ahead in at least 9 of the 12 paired corpora.

  • It helps in 2 of 12 cells: Kannada, alphabetic (34.4% against Finnish 19.4%; +15.1; 9 of 12) and Malayalam, syllabic (10.7% against Latin 3.1%; +7.6; 10 of 12).
  • Malayalam alphabetic adds 10.7 points but is ahead in only 7 of 12 corpora, because several of its runs fail outright.
  • Italian helps nowhere: +6.3 on alphabetic (6 of 12), and below Finnish on syllabic (-1.2) and logo-syllabic scripts (-0.2).
  • Finnish, which is unrelated to both hidden languages, recovers 14.0-19.7% of alphabetic tokens against the relatives' gold. Its transcription shares many letters with ours (a, i, u, k, t, p, n), so coincidence alone reaches that level.

C2. Constant-answer floor. Mapping every sign to the single most common regular reflex scores 14.2-15.8% on alphabetic scripts. The real relatives stay above it there. On logographic scripts the floor is 8.6-17.5% and every real relative scores 0.0%; on syllabic and logo-syllabic scripts, Kannada and Italian are at or below it.

C3. Solver budget. With 10 EM restarts instead of 3 (the first three identical), only Old Tamil-Malayalam, alphabetic, moves by more than 10 points: 30.4% to 41.9%. Kannada alphabetic falls from 32.2% to 28.9%, Italian alphabetic rises from 16.9% to 18.1%, and Latin harsh alphabetic rises from 36.1% to 43.6%. A higher likelihood does not always mean more signs right.

C4. Determinism. The 3-restart reruns reproduce all 120 frozen-run values exactly.

Degeneracy. The share of sign tokens assigned to the single most common predicted unit is 12.4% (Old Tamil) and 12.2% (Latin) with the real relatives, against 11.1% and 11.2% with unrelated references: the solutions do not collapse onto one unit.

Reachability (exploratory, reports/v2/cd/reach_check.md). The entry-2 ceiling counts a token as recoverable if its reflex occurs anywhere in the relative, but the frozen solver only predicts among the reference's 250 most frequent units. On logographic scripts the reachable share is far lower than the ceiling: 25.4% against 51.4% (Italian), 2.3% against 24.3% (Kannada) and 10.1% against 16.9% (Malayalam). On syllabic and alphabetic scripts the cap hardly binds. The v1 sister, cut from the same text as the hidden corpus, shares its frequent words, so the cap rarely binds for it. Logographic comparisons between the synthetic sister and real relatives are therefore partly a solver artefact.

v2.6 What the results show

  1. No real relative brings any script type to a 50% mean (pre-registered, replicated).
    • In the related tier, 7 of the 144 real-relative runs reach 50% (3 relatives, 4 script types, 12 corpora each): 2 on seeds 0-2 and 5 on seeds 3-5, all alphabetic. Single runs reach 66.0% (Malayalam), while every cell mean stays at or below 36.7% (reports/v2/cd/pooled.md).
    • With 10 restarts the highest cell mean on seeds 0-2 is 41.9% (Malayalam, alphabetic).
    • Syllabic and logo-syllabic scripts reach 2.3-10.7% with a real relative over 12 corpora; logographic scripts reach 0.0%.
  2. Most of the alphabetic recovery does not need the relative. An unrelated Finnish reference recovers 14.0-19.7% against the relatives' gold, and a constant answer 14.2-15.8%. The relatives add 6.3-15.1 points over the better unrelated reference on alphabetic scripts, and by the pre-registered rule help measurably in 2 of 12 cells. Italian helps in none.
  3. The Old Tamil alphabetic scores are in the range of the Ugaritic anchor (30.5% and 34.4% against 24.6-29.9% like-for-like), as pre-registered check R2 required. This is a consistency check, not corroboration: the conditions differ (section v2.7).
  4. The bracket does not hold (post hoc; docs/v2_negative_results.md, item 3).
    • Italian falls below harsh on both seed blocks for alphabetic and syllabic scripts (pooled alphabetic 20.3% against 31.5%), and more restarts widen the gap.
    • Kannada falls below harsh for syllabic and logo-syllabic scripts on both seed blocks.
    • The real relatives therefore replace the bracket as the primary evidence for this tier. That reporting decision was taken after seeing the results.
  5. Closeness on the yardsticks did not predict Task D (exploratory; Figure v2.3). Italian is closer to Latin than harsh on every yardstick and agrees better in letter frequencies (unigram JSD 0.026 against 0.059; reports/v2/cd/frequency_check.md), yet scores lower. A text effect of the needed size is documented in the Ugaritic anchor, where a genre-matched Hebrew reference added 5-16 points.
  6. Logographic zeros are partly a solver artefact (exploratory; section v2.5). The solver's 250-unit cap excludes most cognates of the hidden language's frequent words.
  7. Task C rarely finds the real relative (pre-registered check R4, replicated). The revised rule picks it in 0 (Malayalam), 9 and 10 (Kannada) and 6 and 6 (Italian) of 24 corpora on the two seed blocks, mostly preferring Latin or Finnish; the original rule picks Sumerian in all 72 corpora of seeds 0-2.
  8. Single runs are unstable, and budget matters for one cell. Scores for the same relative and script type range from 0.0% to 66.0% between the 12 corpora (Malayalam, alphabetic), and 10 restarts raise Malayalam alphabetic by 11.5 points.

Figure v2.3. Alphabetic Task D (related tier, mean and 95% interval over 12 corpora) against two yardsticks for the same pairs (harsh yardsticks measured on seeds 0-2). Same-script pairs only. Exploratory: five points.

For Indus, where no relative is agreed and the candidates and none tiers are the relevant ones, these results add one point. With these comparison-based solvers, a genuine relative adds little beyond what an unrelated language or a constant answer already gives, outside alphabetic scripts. Inside them the added value is modest and unstable.

v2.7 What these results do not show

  • How hard Indus is. The hidden corpora are synthetic, generated at Indus scale from known languages; the solver is told the script type; and the relative is handed to it. Indus has no agreed relative, so the candidates and none tiers are the relevant ones there.
  • That a real relative is of little use to a human decipherer. These solvers use neither cognates nor sound laws unless given the correspondence table, which is withheld: every score is before the cognate step. A human decipherer can use both; these scores say nothing about that.
  • That the relationship, rather than the text, drives the scores. Each relative is one text of one genre and period. In the Ugaritic anchor, a pre-registered tier with a genre-matched Hebrew reference (the poetic books) raised revised-EM recovery from 32.0% to 37.2% on natural texts, and from 24.6-29.9% to 38.4-43.0% on texts re-cut to mean length 4.6 (reports/v2/ugaritic/results.md). Text effects of that size could account for the bracket failure.
  • A ranking of sister against descendant. There are two relatives of one hidden language, with different texts, periods and reference sizes.
  • Independent confirmation from Ugaritic. The Old Tamil alphabetic scores and the like-for-like Ugaritic range are similar, but the conditions differ. Ugaritic has 6,928 sign tokens in 29 real letters with no synthetic allographs; our corpora have 12,904-13,401 tokens with allographs, homophony and polyvalence (reports/v2/cd/pooled.md). Hebrew letters also cannot match an unrelated reference by coincidence, while Latin-alphabet transcriptions can (control C1). The agreement is a consistency check, not corroboration.
  • Statistical significance. No hypothesis test was pre-registered for these comparisons, and none is reported. Intervals are descriptive.

v2.8 Limitations of this section

  1. Two hidden languages only. Latin and Old Tamil; Old Tamil has both relatives, so the two Dravidian rows share their hidden side and their gold source. Finnish-Estonian is waiting for its gold source and will run on the same frozen code. Sanskrit has no relative with clear terms.
  2. Relationship and text are confounded. Each relative is one text of one genre and period; the effect of the relationship cannot be separated from that of the text.
  3. Old gold sources. Caldwell (1875) and Grandgent (1907) are cited per row, gaps are marked rather than filled, and some details are dated.
  4. Unverified transcriptions. The Wikisource transcriptions state no source edition, and Kannada coverage is partial.
  5. Normalisation choices. Normalisation is fixed in code and tested, but it is a choice, and it moves the Kannada distance a great deal.
  6. Scoring choices.
    • Task D is scored whatever reference the solver chose, as in v1, so a wrong reference can still score by coincidence.
    • The logogram cognate rule (the reflex must occur in the relative) makes logographic ceilings low by construction.
  7. v1 assumptions still apply. The solver is told the script type (an oracle). The Ugaritic gold table is still secondary: Wikipedia, citing Kogan (2011), which we have not read.
  8. Few corpora per cell, with high variance. Six corpora per cell in the primary tables, twelve when pooled with the replication. Single-corpus scores are often bimodal (near 0% or near 50%), and percentile bootstrap intervals over so few corpora understate uncertainty. All intervals here are descriptive.
  9. A convenience sample of pairs. The pairs were chosen for open texts and licences, not as a representative sample of real relationships. Nothing here generalises to "real relatives" at large.
  10. One solver family. All solvers are frequency- or EM-based (after Knight et al. 2006), with a fixed budget. Neural models and human decipherers, who use cognates and sound laws, may do better.
  11. Analysis code outside the freeze guard. The replication and control drivers and the analysis scripts live in reports/v2/cd/analysis/, which the frozen-code check does not cover. They call only frozen functions; each was committed before its run, and the commits are named in the analysis history.
  12. Gold tables spot-checked, not audited. Four rows were rechecked against the scanned sources; the rest rest on the original reading.
  13. The entry-2 ceiling ignores the solver. It counts a token as recoverable if its reflex occurs anywhere in the relative, while the solver can only predict among the reference's 250 most frequent units. For logographic scripts the reported ceilings overstate what the solver could reach (section v2.5). This was found after the run and is reported, not corrected.
