## 4. Calibration problems that limit interpretation

- **Inventory calibration erases script-type signal.** Every corpus is pushed into the Indus range of 400-700 signs. Alphabets (20-60 phonemes) get there only with many allographs per letter, and logographies (thousands of words) only by restricting vocabulary. Any method that infers script type from inventory size is therefore defeated by construction. That is a real property of the Indus problem (inventory size is uninformative once allography is possible), not a bug, but it means the inventory rule's Task B score says nothing about its value on unconstrained scripts.
- **Positional and frequency targets were tuned through text selection**, a preference for windows built from frequent words, ending in frequent words, starting in diverse words. The `holdout` regime leaves these untuned. Section 2 shows how far each corpus drifts without them.
- **Duplicate cap.** The calibrator is not allowed to exceed a 40% duplicate-text share. That number is our design choice, not a published Indus statistic.
- **Corrected targets.** An earlier version calibrated to 5,500 texts and mean 4.4 from the project brief (unverified). All corpora now use the verified M77 figures: 2,906 texts and 4.60 signs per text. `median_length` = 4 is still unverified (tolerance ±1). Plots also shade 1,548-5,500 texts.
